# Practice Exams

This section has two full mock exams and a set of flashcards. Use them to test yourself under exam
conditions and to find the chapters you still need to review.

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

- **[Mock Exam 1](mock-exam-1.md)**: 60 questions, weighted like the official blueprint.
- **[Mock Exam 2](mock-exam-2.md)**: 60 different questions, same weighting.
- **[Rapid-Fire Flashcards](flashcards.md)**: short question-and-answer cards for the last few days.

</div>

## How the mocks are built

The Linux Foundation publishes the domain weights but not the number of questions. Each mock uses 60
questions, the typical size of an associate-level exam, split to match the blueprint:

| Domain | Weight | Questions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. MCP Fundamentals | 16% | 10 |
| 2. Architecture & Components | 14% | 8 |
| 3. Interactions & Execution | 26% | 16 |
| 4. Security & Governance | 24% | 14 |
| 5. Use Cases & Ecosystem | 20% | 12 |

Questions from different domains are mixed together, as on the real exam. Each one has a small domain
tag, so you can tell afterwards which areas cost you points. Most questions are scenarios: they describe
a host, server or deployment and ask what is correct, what is wrong, or what to do next.

Every answer follows the `2026-07-28` specification. Where an SDK behaves differently (the labs point
out a few cases), the spec is the right answer.

## How to take a mock

1. **Set a 90-minute timer.** That gives you about 90 seconds per question.
2. **Answer every question before opening any answer.** Write your letters on paper.
3. **Mark questions you are unsure about** and come back to them if you have time. On the real exam,
   an unanswered question is always wrong, so never leave a blank.
4. **Score yourself** with the answer key at the bottom of the page, and count your misses by domain.
5. **Review every miss, and every lucky guess.** Read the explanation, then the linked chapter
   section. Understanding *why* the distractor was tempting is worth more than the right letter.

## What your score means

| Score | Meaning | Next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **85% or more** (51+) | Ready | Flashcards, then book the exam |
| **75–84%** (45–50) | Borderline | Rework the weakest domain's chapters and its quiz, then take the other mock |
| **Under 75%** (under 45) | Not yet | Go back to the study plan; don't retake the same mock until at least a week has passed |

!!! tip "Take them in order, once each"
    Take Mock 1 at the midpoint of your study plan, to find gaps. Keep Mock 2 for the final week, as a
    readiness check. A retake inflates your score, because you remember the answers rather than the
    reasoning.
